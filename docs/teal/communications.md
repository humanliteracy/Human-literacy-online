# TEAL communication package · proposed copy · October 28, 2026

All copy is for founder review. No post, video upload, organizational contact, paid promotion, or automated distribution is authorized or performed. Narration durations are production estimates; time an actual read before locking captions. The full proposed letter is the canonical article in `teal/index.html` and `teal/letter.html`, with a plain-text extract in `founding-letter.txt`.

## Founding vision / one sentence

TEAL is one person's invitation to practice respect, build useful knowledge, and cooperate toward a world where human dignity and access to life's essentials depend less on wealth.

## Thirty-second introduction

We have learned to fly. We have walked on the Moon. What if our greatest achievement is still ahead of us: learning to care for one another more effectively?

TEAL begins with Respect for Self, Respect for Others, and Respect for Everything. Peace, Love, and Happiness are our aspirations.

Explore the message. Question it. Choose one useful action, if you wish. A world worth building begins with an invitation.

## Ninety-second explanation

We have learned to fly, walked on the Moon, and connected people across oceans. Yet food, essential healthcare, and meaningful education remain beyond reach for many.

What if progress were measured partly by the suffering we prevent and the opportunities we create for one another?

TEAL means Trinity Equals Amazing Love. The three principles are Respect for Self, Respect for Others, and Respect for Everything. No religious, political, or economic belief is required. Peace, Love, and Happiness are aspirations that can strengthen one another.

This begins with one person's invitation. It is not a promise that goodwill alone can solve global problems. Resources are limited. Institutions matter. Solutions must face evidence, costs, disagreement, and the experience of people affected.

Human Literacy connects this vision to practical learning. You can explore ten existing life-skills lessons or try a short, scripted conversation in the Life Simulator. No account or payment is required.

You do not have to agree with TEAL to deserve respect. Read the message. Examine the sources. Consider one action within your capacity. Share it only if it matters to you.

A world worth building is something we can choose to work toward, together.

## Three-minute narrated founding message

To humanity, from one of us.

We have learned to fly. We have walked on the Moon. We have connected people across oceans, found ways to prevent diseases, and shared knowledge across the world.

Yet people still go hungry. Essential healthcare remains out of reach for many. Children grow up without the opportunity to learn what they need for life.

These realities do not erase what humanity has achieved. They show how much of our work remains unfinished.

What if our greatest achievement is still ahead of us?

What if progress meant not only what we invent or possess, but also the suffering we prevent, the freedom we protect, and the opportunities we make possible for one another?

Our lives are not the same. We carry different histories, beliefs, responsibilities, and hopes. Some of us can imagine the distant future. Others are trying to make it through today.

No one should have to earn human dignity through wealth, status, nationality, or agreement with an idea.

TEAL begins with three principles: Respect for Self. Respect for Others. Respect for Everything.

TEAL means Trinity Equals Amazing Love. No religious, political, or economic belief is required. Respect is the practice. Peace, Love, and Happiness are aspirations that can strengthen one another.

Respect includes boundaries, responsibility, and the courage to question harm. It does not require obedience or silence.

Imagine a world where financial hardship no longer leaves people facing preventable hunger. Where essential healthcare is meaningfully accessible. Where learning continues throughout life. Where technology serves people while protecting their choices.

This will take more than goodwill. Resources are limited. Interests compete. Institutions can fail. Useful solutions must be tested, questioned, and improved. Individual action supports, but cannot replace, institutional responsibility.

Human Literacy offers one practical beginning: free life-skills lessons and a short, scripted Life Simulator. The broader work will depend on people and organizations already helping, and on evidence of what is useful.

This starts with one person's invitation. It promises no inevitable success.

You do not have to agree with TEAL to deserve respect.

Read. Reflect. Learn something useful. Choose one realistic action, if you wish. Question what is wrong. Share what matters. You may also simply consider the idea and leave.

For ourselves. For one another. For generations we will never meet.

A world worth building.

## Educational introduction

TEAL offers the values and humanitarian vision. Human Literacy Online offers practical education: ten free interactive life-skills lessons and an optional scripted Life Simulator with private drafting support. Explore listening, boundaries, emotions, asking for help, repairing mistakes, and thoughtful decisions. No account or payment is required. The current Coach uses scripts and rules; it is not live AI or individualized professional care. A broader Everyday Mentor and Universal Life Mentor curriculum remain development work. Participation is voluntary, and learning effectiveness must be evaluated rather than assumed.

## Video production direction

Use the original emblem and clean cosmic artwork; no fabricated scenes of suffering, testimonials, crowds, partnerships, or founder recordings. Quiet human narration is preferred if the founder wishes; do not clone or synthesize a person's voice without appropriate authorization and disclosure. No video/audio has been generated.

Recommended masters: 1080 × 1920 vertical for the short introduction; 1920 × 1080 horizontal for the three-minute message. These are editorial production choices, not universal platform rules. Keep essential text away from interface overlays; crop-test each actual composer. Avoid flashing rays, rapid word-by-word captions, and background audio needed for understanding.

Start on one existing platform, learn from willing readers, and expand only when comprehension, accessibility, and founder capacity justify it.

## Pacing proposal after separate release/posting approval

- Week 1: one personal introduction on one existing platform; 3–5 willing readers explain the message in their own words. Ask what felt confusing, inaccessible, or pressuring. No claim that this small sample represents humanity.
- Week 2: correct the page; share one captioned short video only if useful and separately approved.
- Week 3: prepare one private Spanish translation draft if qualified reviewers are available; no automatic publication or unsolicited outreach.
- Week 4: review deidentified observations and corrections; choose at most one next channel/language. Pause if benefit does not justify the effort.

Proposed founder ceiling: 30–45 minutes per week, adjustable downward. No recurring automation, recruitment, analytics, or schedule was activated. Caring for family, health, and financial security takes priority over distribution quotas.

## Languages and cultural review

Plain English is the current review language. Spanish is the first translation candidate as a practical cross-country extension of an English-source project; qualified reviewers are not yet secured. Then select from Arabic, French, Hindi, Mandarin Chinese (Simplified/Traditional intentionally distinguished), and Portuguese according to actual interest and review capacity. Later consider Bengali, Indonesian, Swahili, and community-requested languages. This is not a ranking of human importance or a calculated combined audience; populations overlap.

Freeze the approved English version and glossary. Use a native bilingual translator, with AI only as a disclosed draft aid; obtain independent bilingual and community cultural review, then 2–3 willing reader comprehension checks and language-specific phone/keyboard/screen-reader/caption checks. Record human approval, version and date before publishing. Flag translations as outdated when source meaning changes. Without sufficient reviewers, keep drafts private.

Keep TEAL as the project name; explain the three principles rather than forcing a translated acronym. Review “Trinity” as a three-part name with no belief requirement; respect as compatible with boundaries/disagreement; love as care rather than compulsory emotion; and “everything” as attention to consequences rather than treating every action as equally right. Avoid U.S.-specific assumptions and label service geography.

Implement reviewed languages with correct BCP 47 `lang`, separate `dir` declarations, logical CSS, adaptable lines and font fallbacks. Test RTL, long labels, CJK and Devanagari; translate navigation, sources, resources, alt text, captions, and metadata. No IP-based nationality/religion assumptions, compulsory location request, or authoritative machine-only translations. Add hreflang only for real reviewed published equivalents. [P10]
